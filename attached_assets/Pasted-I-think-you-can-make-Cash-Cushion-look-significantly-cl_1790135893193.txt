I think you can make Cash Cushion look significantly cleaner without sacrificing the complexity of the application. The screenshots actually give us a good foundation: the interface is already fairly restrained, with a white background, blue chart elements, and recognizable transaction cards.

My recommendation is to build a neutral, finance-oriented design system with one strong blue accent, then use color consistently to communicate meaning. The key is to stop every component from competing for attention.

1. The visual direction

CleanData-denseFinance-oriented

Think of the style as a modern financial control panel: clean enough to scan quickly, but structured enough to support charts, forecasts, transaction lists, merchant filters, and account details.

I’d keep the existing overall layout and focus on improving the visual language rather than redesigning everything from scratch.

3. How I’d apply it to your existing components

Cash forecast chart

Keep the blue line as the primary series. Use a lighter blue or dashed line for forecast values, and a subtle blue tint for selected dates.

Historical balanceForecast

Income and deposits

Use green for the amount and a narrow green left border or icon treatment. Keep the transaction card itself white.

This preserves the positive-income cue without turning the whole card green.

Expenses and bills

Use dark text for merchant names, red for negative amounts, and amber for upcoming or due bills.

Avoid making every expense card red. That would overwhelm a transaction-heavy screen.

Forecast transactions

Use a very light blue card background with a blue border or badge. This makes forecast entries distinguishable from actual posted transactions.

Filters and navigation

White backgrounds, slate text, subtle borders, and blue selected states. Avoid using the primary blue for every icon and label.

One important rule: don’t rely on color alone to communicate whether something is posted, forecast, pending, income, or an expense. Use labels and icons too.
